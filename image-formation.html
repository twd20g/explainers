A cross-section of a camera. Rays from each candle's flame travel in straight lines; only those passing through the opening reach the image plane, where the picture forms upside-down. Move the plane and the image grows or shrinks (magnification = plane distance ÷ object distance). Widen the aperture and every object point spreads into a blur circle — brighter, because more light gets through, but fuzzy. Add a lens and it bends that wide bundle back to a point, so you get brightness *and* sharpness — but only for objects at one distance, so the two candles can't both be crisp at once (depth of field). The chief ray through the centre is undeviated, which is why the lens changes focus but not image position or size.
