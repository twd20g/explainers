The sampler measures the wave at a fixed rate (the ticks on the axis). Below Nyquist — more than two samples per cycle — the dots pin the wave down and it reconstructs exactly. At Nyquist (two samples per cycle) you are at the edge. Above Nyquist the samples are too sparse: they are identical to the samples of a slower wave, so the only thing you can reconstruct is that slower alias — a high frequency masquerading as a low one. Notice that a wave of f cycles and one of 16−f cycles produce the very same dots.
